Mathematical biology beyond cancer

Many of the tools we use in oncology were developed or tested on other biological and environmental problems, and we keep working on them. Examples are a spatial model of plant invasion mediated by allelopathy, in which an invader releases a toxin against the native species and the invasion spreads as a wave; models of dengue transmission, fitted to the incidence data of the city of Campinas with mosquito parameters that depend on temperature and rainfall (with Hyun M. Yang and collaborators); numerical schemes for river flood routing based on an inertial approximation of the Saint-Venant equations; and the modeling of the translocation of a fungicide injected into coconut palms, combined with laboratory measurements. These works share the approach that guides our research in oncology: a mechanistic model, analyzed mathematically and confronted with data.
