Research

We build mathematical models of cancer and its treatment, from the dynamics of cell populations within a tissue to the response of an individual patient. Our work follows two complementary directions. In the first, models are calibrated to clinical data to describe, and predict, how each patient responds to therapy: when the data of a patient are entered into the computer and the model is fitted to them in order to forecast the future, we obtain something like a digital twin of that patient. In the second, we study the mathematics and the biology behind the models: how ecological and evolutionary principles shape tumor growth and resistance, which dynamical behaviors a model can produce, and how its solutions can be analyzed and computed. Our main application areas are leukemias, immunotherapy and the evolution of therapy resistance.

  1. CAR-T cell immunotherapy: modeling and response prediction
  2. Cancer ecology and evolution
  3. Chronic myeloid leukemia: patient-specific models, dose reduction and treatment-free remission
  4. Nonlinear dynamics and bifurcations
  5. PDE models and numerical analysis in mathematical oncology

Line 1

CAR-T cell immunotherapy: modeling and response prediction

CAR-T cells are engineered T lymphocytes, a "living drug" whose kinetics in the patient (expansion, contraction and persistence) differ from those of conventional drugs. Building on previous models developed with Brazilian colleagues, which describe these multiphasic kinetics, tumor resistance through antigen loss and the role of healthy B cells, we now apply and extend them to patients with B-cell lymphomas, together with the group of Malte von Bonin (Dresden University Hospital) and Ingmar Glauche (TU Dresden). We also model the timeline of cytokine release syndrome. The models are calibrated to patient data and used to interpret what shapes the response and to look for early predictors of relapse. A recent finding is that CAR-T kinetics alone do not determine the outcome; tumor-specific features such as burden and antigen density also matter.

Line 2

Cancer ecology and evolution

A tumor is an ecosystem of competing cell types under selection, and treatment is a perturbation of that ecosystem. We use ideas from ecology, evolution and statistical physics, together with mathematical models, to study resilience of the healthy state, evolution of drug resistance, adaptive therapy, phenotypic plasticity and clonal dynamics in hematopoiesis. Recent topics include the design and stability of deterministic and stochastic adaptive-therapy cycles (with Armando Neves and Yuri Vilela), the role of niche-mediated quiescence and heterogeneity in hematopoietic stem cells observed in clonal-tracking data, and a unified view of cellular plasticity as stochastic gradient descent on an epigenetic landscape, in which fitness differences lose long-term relevance under uniform competition. Ongoing work extends these ideas to clonal hematopoiesis. The line began with a resilience perspective on cancer onset and is, for us, a growing area at the interface of mathematics, ecology and biology.

Line 3

Chronic myeloid leukemia: patient-specific models, dose reduction and treatment-free remission

Tyrosine kinase inhibitors control chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) in most patients, but life-long therapy has side effects and high costs, and only about half of the well-responding patients who stop treatment remain in remission. We develop mechanistic models of leukemic stem cells and the immune response, fit them to the molecular response of individual patients (BCR-ABL1 levels) and use them to ask clinical questions: how far can the dose be reduced without losing efficacy, which patients are likely to sustain treatment-free remission (TFR), and which measurements are needed to tell them apart? Our work, done with the group of Ingmar Glauche at TU Dresden, has supported dose de-escalation strategies and a classification of patients by their immunological configuration, and it keeps moving toward simpler models that can be estimated from routine monitoring data. In earlier work on acute promyelocytic leukemia, with Gerson Yoshinari (Hospital das Clínicas de Itajubá) and Eduardo Rego (University of São Paulo), we modeled patient data to compare alternative treatment strategies.

Line 4

Nonlinear dynamics and bifurcations

Mathematical models of biological systems can show multiple stable states, oscillations, abrupt transitions and even chaos. We use the qualitative theory of differential equations (stability, limit cycles, basins of attraction, stable manifolds, bifurcations) to understand which behaviors a model can produce and which parameters control them. Besides the classical Hopf bifurcation, our models display bifurcations of higher codimension, such as Bogdanov-Takens, Bautin, zero-Hopf and triple-zero, and chaotic dynamics associated with Shilnikov homoclinic orbits: a true zoo of dynamical complexity. In oncology, this analysis has explained oscillating tumor load in leukemia as an effect of tumor-immune interaction and oscillatory remissions followed by relapse in CAR-T therapy. We also work on the classical competition models of ecology, with applications to resilience. Collaborations: Denis Braga and Antonio Carlos Zambroni de Souza (UNIFEI) and Claudio Vidal (Universidad del Bío-Bío, Chile).

Line 5

PDE models and numerical analysis in mathematical oncology

Spatial structure, such as tumor invasion into tissue, and population heterogeneity, such as variation in reproduction rates or in antigen expression, lead to partial differential and integro-differential equations. Together with Anderson Araujo (UFV) and collaborators, we study these models rigorously (existence and uniqueness of solutions, optimal control) and complement the analysis with numerical simulations. Examples are acid-mediated tumor invasion with chemotherapy, models with non-local diffusion, generalizations of the Lotka-Volterra competition model with continuous traits, and a model of CAR-T cell resistance in which the antigen expression of tumor cells is a continuous variable, an integro-partial differential equation that we analyze and simulate numerically (with Daniela Santurio). Related interests in the theory of PDEs, nonlinear analysis and control theory are shared with Maicon Sônego (UNIFEI).
