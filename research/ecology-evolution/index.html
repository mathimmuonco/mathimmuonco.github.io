Cancer ecology and evolution

A tumor is an ecosystem of competing cell types under selection, and treatment is a perturbation of that ecosystem. We use ideas from ecology, evolution and statistical physics, together with mathematical models, to study resilience of the healthy state, evolution of drug resistance, adaptive therapy, phenotypic plasticity and clonal dynamics in hematopoiesis. Recent topics include the design and stability of deterministic and stochastic adaptive-therapy cycles (with Armando Neves and Yuri Vilela), the role of niche-mediated quiescence and heterogeneity in hematopoietic stem cells observed in clonal-tracking data, and a unified view of cellular plasticity as stochastic gradient descent on an epigenetic landscape, in which fitness differences lose long-term relevance under uniform competition. Ongoing work extends these ideas to clonal hematopoiesis. The line began with a resilience perspective on cancer onset and is, for us, a growing area at the interface of mathematics, ecology and biology.
