Nonlinear dynamics and bifurcations

Mathematical models of biological systems can show multiple stable states, oscillations, abrupt transitions and even chaos. We use the qualitative theory of differential equations (stability, limit cycles, basins of attraction, stable manifolds, bifurcations) to understand which behaviors a model can produce and which parameters control them. Besides the classical Hopf bifurcation, our models display bifurcations of higher codimension, such as Bogdanov-Takens, Bautin, zero-Hopf and triple-zero, and chaotic dynamics associated with Shilnikov homoclinic orbits: a true zoo of dynamical complexity. In oncology, this analysis has explained oscillating tumor load in leukemia as an effect of tumor-immune interaction and oscillatory remissions followed by relapse in CAR-T therapy. We also work on the classical competition models of ecology, with applications to resilience. Collaborations: Denis Braga and Antonio Carlos Zambroni de Souza (UNIFEI) and Claudio Vidal (Universidad del Bío-Bío, Chile).
